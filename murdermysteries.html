Genre: Murder Mysteries

Book 1: A Good Girl's Guide to Murder

Author: Holly Jackson

Rating: ✮✮✮✮✩ 4.6

A Good Girl's Guide to Murder is a gripping, twist-filled mystery featuring seventeen-year-old Pippa "Pip" Fitz-Amobi, a sharp student who refuses to accept that Sal Singh killed Andie Bell five years ago, setting out to prove his innocence for a school project. The clues and interview logs feel clever, the small-town secrets keep raising the stakes, and the partnership between Pip and Sal's brother Ravi feels genuinely grounded. It is an addictive, fast-moving read that pulls you right in, especially if you love suspenseful mysteries.

Book 2: One of Us is Lying

Author: Karen M. McMannus

Rating: ✮✮✮✮✩ 4.8

One of Us Is Lying is a tense, Breakfast Club-style thriller centered around five high schoolers who walk into detention, but only four walk out alive after the school's gossip app creator, Simon, is murdered. The shifting perspectives keep you guessing, the secrets each student hides raise real suspicion, and the unlikely alliances that form under pressure feel surprisingly authentic. It is a compulsive, page-turning mystery that hooks you from the first chapter, especially if you enjoy high school drama and whodunits.

Book 3: Thin Air

Author: Kellie M. Parker

Rating: ✮✮✮✮✮ 5.0

Thin Air by Kellie M. Parker is a high-stakes, claustrophobic thriller following seventeen-year-old Emily Walters, a boarding school student desperate to win a life-changing scholarship prize to save her family from poverty. When she and eleven other teens board a private jet to Paris, the competition turns lethal, and Emily must navigate deadly challenges while keeping her own dark secrets hidden. The locked-room setting at thirty thousand feet builds relentless tension, the ticking-clock chapters keep the pace flying, and the shifting loyalties among the contestants make everyone a suspect. It is a gripping, fast-paced survival mystery that is tough to put down, especially if you love deadly games and high-altitude suspense.
